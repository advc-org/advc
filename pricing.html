Pricing

advc is free to use. When a product earns money on its own, we ask for 1% of gross revenue — only after the product's lifetime gross passes USD 10,000. Sales inside advland are always $0: the advland store fee covers those.

Worked example: your product earns USD 20,000 lifetime gross. You owe 1% × (20,000 − 10,000) = USD 100.

The full legal wording lives in the EULA.

What counts as one product

  • One product = one game. The base game and all of its DLC count together toward a single USD 10,000 lifetime threshold.
  • Sequels and versions are separate products. “Adventure I” and “Adventure II” each have their own USD 10,000 lifetime threshold.
  • Ports and remasters of the same game are the same product. Their revenue adds to the same threshold.
  • Games embedded in other systems — a game shipped inside another product, kiosk, platform, or bundle sold outside the usual storefronts — are not self-serve pricing. Contact us for terms.

What “gross revenue” means

“Gross revenue” means all amounts you actually receive from players — product sales, DLC, in-app purchases, subscriptions — plus payments you receive from advertising, less refunds and chargebacks.

Deducted: refunds and chargebacks. advland sales revenue never counts toward gross or the threshold at all.

Not deducted: platform and store fees, payment-processing costs, ad-network fees, production or development costs.

Sales inside advland

Revenue earned through sales on advland is $0 forever, at any amount. You pay the advland store fee on those sales; that fee already funds the engine, so the 1% does not apply on top of it.

If your product is sold both on advland and elsewhere (for example on Steam): the advland sales stay $0 and are excluded from gross entirely. The other storefronts' revenue counts normally toward the threshold and the 1%.

Reporting and payment — we trust our users

There are no statements, audits, or paperwork of any kind. When a product's lifetime gross crosses USD 10,000, you self-report and pay the 1% on the amount above the threshold from the dev portal.

How much we can charge is only possible because we trust our users: we ask, you answer honestly, and the rate stays at 1% instead of 5%.

Your fees buy votes on the engine's future

Every fee you pay converts into feature votes in the dev portal (coming soon): the developers funding the engine steer which features get built. Votes never expire.

FAQ

  • Why do we pay at all?
    The 1% funds the engine's development, and the developers who pay steer it through feature votes in the dev portal.
  • Do you verify my numbers?
    No. We rely on honest self-reporting — that trust is the whole bargain.
  • My game earned $9,000 on Steam and $30,000 on advland. What do I owe?
    Nothing yet. The advland revenue is excluded entirely, and the Steam revenue is under the threshold — it's what counts toward the threshold.
  • Same example, but Steam earned $15,000?
    1% × (15,000 − 10,000) = USD 50. The advland revenue still doesn't count.
  • Do votes expire?
    No.
  • What if I never pay?
    Nothing automated. We ship an engine people can honor a 1% on; if someone won't, we'd rather learn that in conversation than enforce it.
